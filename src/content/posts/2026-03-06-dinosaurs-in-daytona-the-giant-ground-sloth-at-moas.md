Most people drive right past the Museum of Arts and Sciences (MOAS) on their way to the beach. That's a mistake. Because tucked inside one of Volusia County's oldest cultural institutions is a skeleton that makes every dinosaur exhibit at Disney look like a participation trophy.

Meet *Eremotherium laurillardi* — a giant ground sloth that roamed the land that would become Daytona Beach roughly 130,000 years ago.

**It's Enormous. Like, Genuinely Enormous.**

We're talking about a creature that stood over 17 feet tall when it reared up on its hind legs. The fossil was discovered right here in Florida in 1975, and MOAS has been the proud steward of this full skeleton ever since. Standing next to it, you realize the Pleistocene epoch was not messing around.

**But Wait — There's Way More**

The prehistoric Florida wing is just one room in a museum that punches well above its weight. MOAS also houses:

- A Cuban Museum with one of the largest collections of Cuban art outside of Havana
- A Dow Gallery of American Art with work spanning three centuries
- A Chinese export art collection that rivals anything in the Southeast
- A Planetarium that does regular shows for kids and adults

**The Practical Stuff**

MOAS is located at 352 S Nova Rd, Daytona Beach, and it's open Tuesday through Sunday. Admission is affordable, parking is free, and they do rotating exhibits that bring in new reasons to come back.

If you haven't been since your third-grade field trip, go again. You were too busy making up stories about the sloth to actually appreciate it. Now you'll see it differently.

**The Local Take**

This is exactly the kind of place that separates the people who *live* in Daytona from the people who just visit it. Tourists hit the beach and the Speedway. Locals know that the best things here aren't always on the waterfront.

MOAS is a world-class institution in a city that sometimes forgets to brag about its best features. Time to fix that.
