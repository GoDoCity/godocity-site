Deltona residents hoping to celebrate the holidays at the city's annual Christmas parade will have to make other plans.

The city has canceled its Dec. 12 parade, citing anticipated revenue losses following the passage of Florida's Amendment 3 earlier this month.

City Manager Doc Dougherty announced the decision Saturday, warning that the financial impact could extend well beyond the holiday season. Nearly all city-sponsored events are effectively canceled, he said, with the exception of two 5K races that pay for themselves.

The announcement came just six days before the parade's registration deadline, leaving prospective participants and families with an unexpected change in holiday plans.

### Deltona Facing Millions in Budget Cuts

Dougherty estimates the city will need to cut approximately $12 million from its budget in 2027, followed by another $5 million in 2028. By 2030, the cumulative reductions could exceed $23 million.

Deltona faces a particular challenge because much of its tax base comes from residential properties rather than commercial development. With fewer businesses contributing property tax revenue, changes to residential property taxes could have an outsized effect on the city's finances.

Amendment 3 passed statewide on Nov. 3 with 61.98% of the vote.

While the measure received broad voter support, local governments are now assessing what the resulting revenue reductions could mean for their budgets.

### Could Other Volusia County Cities Be Next?

Deltona's decision raises questions for other communities across Volusia County.

Daytona Beach, Ormond Beach, Port Orange, DeLand and neighboring municipalities all depend on property taxes to help fund local operations, although their revenue sources and financial circumstances vary.

Whether those cities will face similar cuts to community events or public services remains unclear.

For Deltona, the cancellation of the Christmas parade is an immediate consequence of the city's anticipated financial challenges. But the larger concern is what other programs and services could be affected in the coming years.

### What Happens Now?

Questions remain about which additional events will be eliminated and whether community organizations might be able to take over some of the city's traditional celebrations.

It is also unclear how the cancellation will affect groups that have already made preparations to participate in the parade.

For families who attend the event each year, the cancellation means losing a familiar holiday tradition. For city officials, it may be the beginning of a much more difficult conversation about what Deltona can afford.
