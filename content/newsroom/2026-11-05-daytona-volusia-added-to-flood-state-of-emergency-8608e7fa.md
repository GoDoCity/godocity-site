## Why it matters

A state of emergency directly affects Volusia residents, and a rising St. Johns River threatens flooding across the county. This is critical public-impact news that readers need to know about regardless of its downbeat tone.

## What happened

Gov. Ron DeSantis expanded Florida's fall flooding state of emergency on Wednesday, Nov. 4, 2026, to include Volusia County and six others. The move follows historic rainfall in Brevard County on Monday — over two feet in a 1-in-1,000-year event — which is now draining into the slowly north-flowing St. Johns River, which is still rising through Seminole, Lake and Volusia counties. The order activates state response plans and the Florida National Guard.

## Key facts

- DeSantis expanded the emergency declaration Wednesday to include Volusia among seven added counties
- More than 2 feet of rain fell in Brevard Monday in a 1-in-1,000-year event
- Water is draining into the southern St. Johns River, which flows north through Volusia
- Order activates state response plans, the Florida National Guard and Florida State Guard
