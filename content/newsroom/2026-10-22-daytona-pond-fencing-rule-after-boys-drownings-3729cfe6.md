## Why it matters

The tragic drowning of two young boys has pushed city commissioners toward a possible new rule requiring fences or barriers around retention ponds, which would affect property owners and developers citywide. It's a public-safety story with real policy consequences and strong community interest.

## What happened

Following the drowning deaths of Kashh Williams, 3, and Elijah Hawkins, 5, in a retention pond at their apartment complex, Daytona Beach commissioners are set to discuss requiring fences or other barriers around retention ponds. Current rules only mandate fencing in certain slope conditions. Commissioner Ken Strickland backs additional protections and could direct staff to draft an ordinance. The investigation into the deaths remains ongoing with no charges filed and no signs of foul play.

## Key facts

- Two boys, ages 3 and 5, drowned in a retention pond at their apartment complex
- City does not currently require fencing around all retention ponds
- State rules only require fencing above certain bank slopes
- Commissioner Ken Strickland supports new fencing requirements
- Commission could direct staff to draft an ordinance
- Investigation ongoing; no charges filed, no signs of foul play
