## Why it matters

E-bikes and scooters are everywhere in beach towns, and these rules would change where riders—especially kids—can go on Flagler Avenue, sidewalks and parks. With $150 fines and possible device seizure, the ordinance directly affects residents, visitors and families. It taps a hot-button safety debate many locals have strong feelings about.

## What happened

The New Smyrna Beach City Commission is considering Ordinance 44-26, which would ban bicycles, e-bikes, scooters and similar devices from sidewalks in key parking and business districts, Flagler Avenue, shopping centers and city parks and recreational courts. Violations would carry a $150 fine plus court costs, with repeat offenses risking device seizure. Riders ages 16-17 would be required to wear helmets, and rental/shared mobility staging on public property would be banned. A well-attended public forum led by the police chief drew resident input, with some urging rider education alongside restrictions.

## Key facts

- Ordinance 44-26 restricts e-bikes, scooters and similar devices on sidewalks in business/parking districts and Flagler Avenue
- Devices would be banned in city parks, trails and on basketball, tennis and pickleball courts
- First violation carries a $150 fine plus court costs; repeat violations could lead to device seizure
- Riders ages 16-17 would be required to wear helmets
- Rental/shared mobility operators barred from staging devices on public property
- Commission set to consider ordinance on second reading with a public hearing
