## Why it matters

Data centers are a hot national land-use and environmental topic, and Volusia just drew a firm line barring the biggest ones from unincorporated areas. It touches local concerns about the aquifer, sinkholes, energy costs and quality of life — issues residents increasingly care about.

## What happened

The Volusia County Council voted 6-0 on Tuesday to prohibit large-scale data centers (monthly peak load of at least 50 megawatts) in unincorporated parts of the county. The ordinance bars the county from accepting or approving any applications, rezonings, comp plan amendments, permits or site plans for such facilities, including expansions of existing ones. Councilman Don Dempsey cited aquifer and sinkhole risks in West Volusia. The 50-MW threshold matches Florida's SB-484 definition, effective July 1, 2026.

## Key facts

- Unanimous 6-0 County Council vote on Tuesday
- Ban applies to data centers with monthly peak load of 50+ megawatts
- Covers unincorporated county land only
- Also blocks expansions that would exceed the threshold
- 50-MW threshold mirrors Florida SB-484, effective July 1, 2026
- Councilman Dempsey cited aquifer/sinkhole risks in West Volusia
