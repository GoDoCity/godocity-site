## Why it matters

Beach erosion directly affects Volusia residents' property, public walkways, and the tourism economy. A $25M taxpayer-funded project showing visible erosion weeks after completion raises real questions about public money and coastal protection strategy.

## What happened

King tides carved near-5-foot cliffs and washed away much of the sand from a recently completed $25M placement project in New Smyrna Beach. County officials defend it as a deliberate 'sacrificial buffer' meant to absorb tide damage, while some residents question whether the money was well spent. The county is pursuing a long-term federal project with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, with council votes expected in early 2027.

## Key facts

- $25M sand placement project recently completed in New Smyrna Beach
- King tides carved cliffs nearly 5 feet tall and re-exposed seawalls
- County lost 6+ million cubic yards of sand after 2022 hurricanes
- County pursuing federal Army Corps project with $0 local cost share after hurricanes
- County council votes expected early 2027; agreement sought before year's end
- Officials say displaced sand may migrate back as wave conditions settle
