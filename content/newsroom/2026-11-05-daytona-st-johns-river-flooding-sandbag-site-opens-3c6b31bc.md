## Why it matters

Rising river water and an expanded state of emergency are a real public-safety and property concern for Volusia residents along the St. Johns, even though local rainfall was light. It tells readers near the river to prepare now and where to get free sandbags, qualifying under the critical-public-impact exception.

## What happened

The St. Johns River is rising in Volusia County as water flows north from heavier-rained areas to the south. Gov. DeSantis expanded the state of emergency to include Volusia, Lake and Seminole counties on Wednesday. Water near Lake Beresford in DeLand has already spread beyond normal shorelines. County officials expect peak impact toward the end of the week and are urging residents near the river to fill sandbags and stock several days of supplies. A free self-serve sandbag site opened at Fire Rescue Station 36 in Osteen, open daily 8 a.m.-5 p.m.
