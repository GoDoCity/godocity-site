## Why it matters

The ordinance makes a decade-old community program a permanent part of city policy, affecting how DeBary handles its free-roaming cat population and public health. It's an uplifting human-interest and animal-welfare story tied to a retiring councilmember's legacy, which readers tend to enjoy and share.

## What happened

DeBary's City Council voted to codify its trap-neuter-release (TNR) program into a permanent permit ordinance, capping roughly a decade of work by Councilmember Patricia Stevenson, who terms out in a few months. Volunteers trap free-roaming cats, the Central Florida Community Pet Clinic neuters them, and they are returned to where they were found. Volunteers have trapped 160 cats or kittens so far this year.

## Key facts

- City Council voted to make the TNR program a permanent permit ordinance
- Program has operated about 10 years without being codified
- Councilmember Patricia Stevenson, program founder, terms out in a few months
- Central Florida Community Pet Clinic handles the surgeries
- 160 cats/kittens trapped so far this year
- Stevenson launched a supporting nonprofit and volunteer base
