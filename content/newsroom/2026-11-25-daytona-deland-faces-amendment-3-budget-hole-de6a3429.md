## Why it matters

Amendment 3's expanded homestead exemption could cost DeLand millions, forcing choices between higher taxes, new fees, or service cuts to police, fire and parks. These decisions directly affect residents' wallets and city services, making it real money and real impact for West Volusia readers.

## What happened

At a Nov. 9 special meeting, DeLand city staff told commissioners the city faces a $5.2 million general-fund shortfall in FY2027-28, growing to $8.8 million the following year, as Amendment 3 raises the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. Staff presented pages of possible new revenue options, including raising the millage rate and adding a fire services fee, and asked commissioners to rate each 'advance, hold, or remove.' It was the first of seven planned off-week budget meetings running through May.

## Key facts

- $5.2M shortfall projected for FY2027-28, growing to $8.8M when fully in effect
- Amendment 3 raises nonschool homestead exemption to $150K (2027) and $250K (2028)
- DeLand's current millage is 6.1841, legally can rise to 10 mills
- Options include raising millage and a new fire services fee
- Seven special budget meetings planned Nov. through May
- Many attendees at Nov. 9 meeting were city employees worried about jobs
