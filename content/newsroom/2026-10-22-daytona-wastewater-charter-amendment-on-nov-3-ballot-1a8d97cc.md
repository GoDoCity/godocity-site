## Why it matters

This directly affects every Daytona Beach resident's drinking water and gives voters a say on whether treated wastewater can ever enter the public supply or aquifer. It's a real ballot decision with health and money implications, making it genuinely useful civic information ahead of Election Day.

## What happened

City commissioners voted in March to put a charter amendment before Daytona Beach voters. On Nov. 3, voters will decide whether to prohibit the city from approving treated wastewater (blackwater) as a direct source for drinking water and from injecting it into the underground aquifer. Existing reclaimed-water use for irrigation would still be allowed.

## Key facts

- Vote is Nov. 3, 2026 for Daytona Beach voters
- Amendment would bar treated wastewater as a direct drinking-water source
- Also bars injecting blackwater into the aquifer
- Existing reclaimed water for irrigation stays allowed
- Commissioners voted in March to put it on the ballot
