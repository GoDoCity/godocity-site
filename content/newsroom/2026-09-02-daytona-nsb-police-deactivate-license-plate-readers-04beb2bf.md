## Why it matters

License plate readers touch on privacy, policing and public safety debates that residents care about, and their sudden deactivation is a meaningful change in how the city polices. The department credits the technology with catching burglars, a shooting suspect and a carjacker, so turning it off raises real questions about local crime response.

## What happened

As of Monday, Aug. 31, 2026, the New Smyrna Beach Police Department deactivated all automated license plate readers in the city while reviewing recent State of Florida memorandums on the technology. The department said it will comply with state guidelines, noted the readers have been used since 2019 with zero misuse allegations, and cited cases where the tech helped catch suspects.

## Key facts

- All ALPRs in New Smyrna Beach deactivated as of Aug. 31, 2026
- Prompted by review of State of Florida memorandums on plate reader use
- Technology in use in NSB since 2019 with zero misuse allegations claimed
- Credited with catching vehicle burglars, a shooting suspect, a carjacker and hit-and-run vehicles
- Department says it will comply with state plans, including equipment in state rights-of-way
