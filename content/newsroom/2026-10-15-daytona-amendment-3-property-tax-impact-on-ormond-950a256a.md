## Why it matters

This is real money in residents' pockets — the amendment could zero out city property taxes for the majority of Ormond Beach homesteaders, while slashing roughly a third of the city's property tax revenue. Homeowners want to know what their bill will look like, and taxpayers care about the services that funding supports. It's a high-impact fiscal story ahead of the vote.

## What happened

The City of Ormond Beach published factual information explaining proposed Amendment 3, which would raise the homestead exemption on non-school property taxes to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. Because the average homesteaded taxable value in Ormond is $237,350, an estimated 7,412 of 12,561 homesteaded properties (59%) would owe no city property tax by 2028. The city projects a revenue loss of about $5.0 million in 2027 and $8.2 million by 2028 — roughly a 32% cut — with no recurring state funding to offset it, raising questions about future service levels.

## Key facts

- Exemption on non-school taxes would rise to $150K in 2027, $250K in 2028
- Average Ormond homesteaded taxable value is $237,350
- Est. 59% of homesteaded properties (7,412 of 12,561) would owe no city property tax by 2028
- City projects ~$5.0M revenue loss in 2027, $8.2M by 2028 — about 32%
- School taxes are unaffected; most owners still get a bill
- Nov 2026 bills unchanged; first changes appear on 2027 TRIM notices
