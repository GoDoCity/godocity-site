## Why it matters

The audit concerns how Daytona Beach spent taxpayer money, including building permit fees and purchasing cards. Residents and builders care whether their fees were misused and how city leaders respond. It's a significant accountability story about city finances.

## What happened

The Florida Auditor General sent Daytona Beach eight preliminary findings from an audit of building permit fees and other spending. Findings say the city used some permit revenue for expenses not tied to code enforcement and retained more fees than state law allows, with additional concerns over purchasing card controls, documentation, and travel expenses. The city has 30 days to respond; Mayor Derrick Henry said the city plans to respond within a week and has already addressed seven of eight findings.

## Key facts

- Florida Auditor General issued eight preliminary findings
- Audit was ordered by lawmakers in December 2025 over spending concerns
- Findings cite misuse/over-retention of building permit fees
- Concerns also raised over purchasing cards, documentation, travel expenses
- City has 30 days to respond; Mayor Henry says response within a week
- Final report to go to Legislature's Joint Legislative Auditing Committee early next year
