## Why it matters

A state operational audit flagging eight issues around permit fees, purchasing cards, travel expenses and financial oversight speaks to how taxpayer money is managed in Daytona Beach. Residents care about accountability and whether their city handles finances responsibly.

## What happened

Daytona Beach received preliminary and tentative findings from the Florida Auditor General's operational audit covering October 2024 through February 2026. The eight issues relate to building permit fee revenue, purchasing card procedures, employee travel expenses and financial oversight. The city says it cooperated and had already implemented stronger internal controls and policy updates. A formal written city response is pending, after which a final report will be issued and presented to the Joint Legislative Auditing Committee early next year.

## Key facts

- Audit period: October 2024 through February 2026
- Eight preliminary issues flagged by Florida Auditor General
- Areas include permit fee revenue, purchasing cards, travel expenses, financial oversight
- These are preliminary, not final findings
- City says it implemented controls before/during audit
- Final report and legislative presentation expected early next year
