## Why it matters

The St. Johns River is approaching major to near-record flood crests in Volusia County, including DeLand and Astor, with water staying elevated for weeks to months. This is a genuine public-safety and property-impact story for west Volusia residents, meeting the critical-impact exception to the fun filter.

## What happened

The St. Johns River Water Management District warned Friday that the river will reach major, near-record or record crests across Brevard, Seminole, Volusia and Lake counties after over 28 inches of rain. Near DeLand, levels were forecast to hit 6 feet by Tuesday (major to near-record); at Astor, 4.1 feet by Saturday (major). Flooding could persist for weeks to months, with Cocoa already surpassing a 1953 record.

## Key facts

- River near DeLand forecast to reach 6 feet Tuesday — major to near-record crest
- Astor forecast to hit 4.1 feet Saturday, major crest, elevated one to two months
- Over 28 inches of rain in some areas drove the flooding
- Cocoa already broke a 1953 record flood level
- SJRWMD storing ~60 billion gallons in Upper St. Johns Basin
