## Why it matters

Amendment 3's expanded homestead exemptions will cost Deltona nearly $30 million over two years, and how the city fills that gap directly affects residents' tax bills and services. The commission's 7-0 rejection of a package of new special assessments is real money in homeowners' pockets and a preview of debates coming to every Volusia city.

## What happened

At a Nov. 16 special meeting, Deltona's City Commission voted 7-0 to deny the city manager's request to begin advertising a 'notice of intent' to add new non-ad valorem special assessments to tax bills. The proposed fees would have covered fire operations, parks, street paving, streetlighting, right-of-way work and municipal facilities to offset revenue lost under voter-approved Amendment 3, which raises the homestead exemption to $150,000 effective Jan. 1, 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.

## Key facts

- Amendment 3 passed with 62% statewide; raises homestead exemption to $150K in 2027, $250K in 2028
- Deltona projects nearly $30 million in lost property-tax revenue over two years
- Commission voted 7-0 Nov. 16 to deny advertising a notice of intent for new special assessments
- Proposed assessments covered fire, parks, street paving, streetlighting and municipal facilities
- Notice of intent must be filed with property appraiser and tax collector before Jan. 1
- Commissioner Brandy White moved to deny; Dory Howington seconded
