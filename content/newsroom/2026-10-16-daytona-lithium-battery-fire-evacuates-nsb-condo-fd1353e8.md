## Why it matters

Lithium-ion battery fires are a growing safety concern as devices proliferate, and this is a concrete local example with a practical takeaway for residents. The fire forced evacuation of a condo building and drew four fire agencies, showing real community impact. Readers can learn charging safety from it.

## What happened

On the night of October 14, 2026, a lithium-ion battery charging on a chair overheated and caught fire inside a unit at Diamond Head Condos in New Smyrna Beach. The building's sprinkler system had already activated when crews arrived to heavy smoke; a second alarm was called and the fire was contained to the unit. Smoke and water damage forced most residents to evacuate, and crews from four agencies responded.

## Key facts

- Fire started at 8:45 p.m. Oct. 14, 2026 at Diamond Head Condos
- Lithium-ion battery charging on a chair ignited
- Four agencies responded: NSB, Volusia County, Edgewater, Port Orange
- Sprinkler system activated; fire contained to the unit
- Most residents evacuated due to smoke and water damage
