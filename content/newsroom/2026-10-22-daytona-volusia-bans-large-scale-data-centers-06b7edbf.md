## Why it matters

Data centers are a hot national land-use fight, and Volusia just drew a line in unincorporated areas over water, electricity and noise concerns. It affects future growth, utility demand and tax-base debates that touch every resident, even though cities keep their own authority.

## What happened

The Volusia County Council approved an ordinance banning large-scale data centers—those with an anticipated monthly peak electrical load of at least 50 megawatts—in unincorporated areas. The vote followed a unanimous first reading Oct. 6; the county has refused new applications since Aug. 17. Officials cited concerns about water and electricity use, noise, grid strain and aquifer impacts, while some members and residents questioned whether a ban or tighter regulation is the better approach.

## Key facts

- Ban applies only to unincorporated Volusia, not cities
- Threshold: 50-megawatt anticipated monthly peak electrical load
- First reading passed unanimously Oct. 6
- No new applications accepted since Aug. 17
- Concerns: water, electricity, noise, grid, aquifer
- Councilman Johansson favored regulation over a ban, citing jobs/tax revenue
