## Why it matters

Port Orange's Colony in the Wood retirement community was among the hardest-hit flood areas, and lingering standing water now raises mosquito-borne illness risk amid Florida's record dengue outbreak. This is a public-health and public-impact story affecting vulnerable residents, with a recurring local flooding grievance tied to nearby development.

## What happened

After record rain and flooding across Central Florida, health officials warned that receding floodwaters leave standing water that breeds mosquitoes. Florida is experiencing a record dengue outbreak (669 locally acquired cases, mostly Tampa Bay area; Volusia not cited with cases). In Port Orange, residents of the Colony in the Wood retirement community reported 35 inches of water on one street and repeated flooding they blame on drainage and nearby development runoff.

## Key facts

- Colony in the Wood retirement community in Port Orange hit hard; one resident reported 35 inches on his street
- Resident says area flooded three times in five years (Ian, Milton, recent storm)
- Florida at record dengue: 669 locally acquired cases, mostly Hillsborough/Pinellas
- Central Florida cases: Orange 16, Seminole 2, Brevard 1; no Volusia cases cited
- Officials urge dumping standing water, bug spray, long sleeves; aerial spraying less effective with standing water/cool temps
